Frequently Asked Questions

Why does UsageClock show different times than macOS Screen Time?
That's by design. macOS Screen Time counts every app that's open - even in the background. UsageClock only tracks the app you're actively focused on. If Chrome is open but you're working in Xcode, only Xcode gets the time.
Why did tracking stop while I was working?
UsageClock pauses tracking after a period of no mouse or keyboard activity (default: 5 minutes). This prevents idle time from being counted as active usage. You can adjust the idle timeout in Settings.
How do I exclude an app from tracking?
Open the dropdown panel, click the gear icon to open Settings, then add the app under "Excluded Apps." You can type a bundle ID or pick from currently running apps.
When does the daily data reset?
All usage data resets at midnight each day. Previous days' data is not retained - UsageClock is focused on your current day only.
Does UsageClock work when my Mac is sleeping?
No. Tracking automatically stops when your Mac goes to sleep or the screen locks, and resumes when you wake it. This ensures only real active usage is counted.
How do I set UsageClock to launch at login?
Open Settings from the dropdown panel and toggle "Launch at login" on.
What is the "Peak" time shown at the bottom?
Peak shows the one-hour window where your total app usage was highest today. It reflects when you were most actively working, regardless of which app you used.

What we collect

App usage data

UsageClock records which apps you use and for how long each day. This data is stored locally on your Mac using SwiftData and is never transmitted anywhere.

What is not collected

  • No account or personal information
  • No browsing history or URLs
  • No keystrokes or mouse activity content
  • No screen content or screenshots
  • No location data
  • No crash reports or analytics

How data is stored

All data is stored locally in your Mac's Application Support folder using Apple's SwiftData framework. The data is scoped to your user account and is not accessible to other users or applications.

Data resets automatically at midnight each day. No historical data is retained beyond the current day.

Input event monitoring

UsageClock monitors global mouse and keyboard events solely to detect idle periods (no activity for a configurable duration). The content of keystrokes is never read or recorded - only the timestamp of the most recent input event is used to determine whether you are actively at your computer.

Third parties

UsageClock does not use any third-party SDKs, analytics services, advertising networks, or crash reporting tools. The app makes no network requests of any kind.

Deleting your data

To delete all stored usage data, remove the following folder:

~/Library/Containers/com.ghost3d.UsageClock/

Or simply uninstall UsageClock - no data remains on your system after removal.
